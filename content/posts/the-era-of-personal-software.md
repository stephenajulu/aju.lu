Hey guys! How are you doing? Hope you are healthy, focused, and stacking wins.

Today I want to talk about a major seismic shift that is about to tear through the software industry: the collapse of the rented software monopoly, and the dawn of **Personal Software**.

For the past twenty years, the technology sector has operated almost exclusively under the rental model: Software-as-a-Service (SaaS). We stopped owning our applications and became permanent tenants. 

Every single digital tool—from your daily note-taking app to customer support dashboards, project management boards, and inventory trackers—turned into a recurring monthly credit card charge. Your data was sucked into proprietary databases hosted in remote cloud silos, locked behind export restrictions and monthly tier upgrades.

This rental model created an absurdly bloated B2B ecosystem. Today, early-stage companies and solo creators easily bleed thousands of dollars every month for a patchwork of twenty different SaaS tools that barely communicate with one another.

We are standing on the precipice of the **SaaSpocalypse**. The era of the bloated, generic B2B SaaS monopoly is coming to a rapid end, replaced by the rise of bespoke, sovereign personal software.

---

### The Economic Collapse of Generic SaaS

Why did businesses agree to pay $50 or $100 per user every month for a project management board, an invoicing app, or a basic CRM? 

Historically, they did it because building custom software was prohibitively expensive. You had to hire product managers, UI/UX designers, backend engineers, and DevOps specialists. Custom development was a luxury reserved exclusively for Fortune 500 corporations.

Generative AI and modern agentic engineering destroy this economic calculation overnight.

Today, an operations manager, an architect, or an accountant can describe their exact organizational workflow, data constraints, and business logic to an AI coding assistant and generate a fully customized, responsive web application in thirty minutes.

If a founder can spin up a bespoke customer database perfectly fitted to their regional logistics workflow in an afternoon—with zero technical debt and zero monthly per-seat licensing fees—why on earth would they pay $2,000 a month to rent a bloated, generic CRM that requires three weeks of onboarding webinars just to learn?

The software itself is being rapidly commoditized. The economic value is shifting completely away from generic application wrappers and returning directly to where it always belonged: **the owner's proprietary data and personal context**.

---

### The Concept of Disposable Software

Personal software introduces a radically liberating paradigm: **disposable software**.

In the traditional corporate software model, software must be engineered as a permanent, multi-tenant monolith built to scale to millions of users. It has to cater to thousands of competing edge cases, international compliance frameworks, and corporate IT requirements. This makes commercial SaaS sluggish, complex, and frustratingly rigid to adapt.

Disposable software is built for a specific, immediate problem, for a single person, or for a small project team:

```
Traditional SaaS:
  [Massive Monolith] ===> [Rigid Templates] ===> [Pay Forever]

Disposable Personal Software:
  [Prompt Intent] ===> [Bespoke App] ===> [Solve Task] ===> [Archive or Delete]
```

Imagine you are executing a two-month marketing campaign with unique regional payment tracking and local inventory constraints. Under the old SaaS paradigm, you'd spend weeks researching tools, signing up for trial plans, and trying to shoehorn your custom workflow into a rigid vendor template.

Under the personal software paradigm, you simply generate a customized dashboard for that specific campaign. You run it locally for sixty days, accomplish your objective, extract your clean CSV/SQLite data, and delete or archive the code when you're done. 

The software is treated like a scrap paper calculation: cheap, fast, hyper-tailored, and completely disposable.

This level of customization restores human dignity and cognitive focus. You no longer force your brain to adapt to the arbitrary quirks of a commercial software vendor; **the software adapts to you**.

### Reclaiming Software Sovereignty

In my engineering lab and software consulting practice in Nairobi, I view personal software as one of the greatest leaps toward genuine digital sovereignty in our lifetime.

When you generate and host your own tools:
1. **You eliminate foreign SaaS subscriptions:** IT expenses shift from volatile foreign currency recurring debts to predictable, local one-time assets.
2. **You reclaim absolute custody of your data:** Customer records, internal workflows, and financial metrics never leave your physical perimeter.
3. **You own your workflows:** No corporate product manager can push an unrequested UI overhaul that breaks your team's muscle memory overnight.

Instead of deploying these personal tools to expensive cloud hyperscalers that monitor your traffic, deploy them to a quiet, $200 Mini PC or homelab node sitting on your desk. 

Software should be an authentic extension of your intellect, not a rented utility on a corporate leash. The era of the SaaS landlord is expiring. The era of personal, sovereign software has arrived.

Stack your tools. Own your iron. Keep building.
